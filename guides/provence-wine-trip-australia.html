How to Plan a Provence Wine Trip from Australia

A practical way to choose a base, group winery visits by area and return home with better memories—not an impossible cross-region itinerary.

In brief: Do not treat Provence as one compact cellar-door district. Choose one or two neighbouring wine areas, stay close to them, and book no more than two substantial estate visits in a day. Confirm appointments directly, arrange a non-driving tasting plan, and check current Australian Border Force rules before bringing bottles home.

Provence wine country stretches across coastal and inland landscapes rather than forming one neat loop. A trip becomes much better when it is organised around place: Cassis and Bandol for coastal whites and structured reds; Aix and Sainte-Victoire for limestone country and all three colours; La Londe for maritime Côtes de Provence; or the Var interior for quieter estates and Coteaux Varois en Provence.

This guide is an orientation tool, not a live booking directory. Estate hours, experiences and access can change with the season and harvest. Use the official route planner and contact each winery directly before locking in transport.

Start with the wine, then choose the base

The most common planning mistake is choosing a famous town first and scattering winery pins across the map afterwards. Reverse the order. Decide which styles or appellations you most want to understand, group nearby estates, and then choose accommodation that reduces driving.

  • For first-time regional context: combine one broad Côtes de Provence or Coteaux d'Aix visit with a more specialised appellation.
  • For red-wine depth: centre the trip on Bandol and look for Mourvèdre-led reds alongside rosé and white.
  • For white wine and the coast: use Cassis as the anchor, then compare with Bandol or a Rolle-led Côtes de Provence white.
  • For terroir comparisons: explore Sainte-Victoire, La Londe or another named Côtes de Provence geographical designation rather than treating the whole appellation as uniform.
  • For a quieter pace: stay inland and explore Coteaux Varois en Provence or central Var estates rather than crossing the coast every day.

A realistic day

One morning visit, lunch, and one afternoon visit is usually more useful than racing through four tasting rooms. Leave time to hear the producer, compare cuvées, eat properly and travel without pressure.

How to book a worthwhile winery visit

The official Vins de Provence wine-tourism page directs travellers to the Route des Vins de Provence planner, which lists hundreds of participating winemakers and cooperatives. Use it to build a shortlist, then verify every detail on the estate's own site or by direct contact.

  • Ask what the visit includes: a tasting counter, vineyard walk, cellar tour and seated masterclass are different experiences.
  • Confirm the language: ask whether the visit is available in English if your group needs it.
  • Check the current schedule: opening hours can narrow outside peak season and during harvest.
  • Disclose the group size: even a small group may need an appointment.
  • Ask about access: confirm parking, stairs, walking surfaces, shade and accessibility where relevant.
  • Choose contrast: pair a cooperative with an estate, or compare coastal and inland sites, instead of booking nearly identical visits.

Bring a simple question framework: Which appellation is this? Which grapes are in the wine? What does this site contribute? How was the rosé, red or white made? When should the bottle be opened? The glossary and tasting method help turn a visit into a useful record.

Plan transport before the tasting

Many estates are rural, and public transport does not connect every cellar door. Decide who is not drinking, book a licensed driver or guide, or choose a visit that can be reached safely from your base. Do not improvise the return journey after tasting.

Spitting is normal in a professional tasting and lets you compare wines more clearly. Water, food and slower pacing still matter. If you are driving, the safest tasting plan is not to consume alcohol at all; local drink-driving rules and enforcement apply regardless of what feels like a small pour.

Summer traffic, market days and narrow village roads can change the rhythm of a route. Allow more time than a map estimate suggests, and avoid making a restaurant reservation or second visit dependent on a perfect drive.

Bringing Provence wine back to Australia

At the time this guide was checked, Australian Border Force stated that travellers aged 18 or over could bring 2.25 litres of alcoholic beverages duty free in accompanied baggage. If the allowance is exceeded, duty and tax can apply to all alcohol in that category, not only the excess. Rules can change, so read the current official guidance before travelling and declare when required.

  • Keep purchase records and photograph the front and back labels.
  • Ask the estate for suitable protective packaging; place bottles in checked baggage unless current aviation rules say otherwise.
  • Avoid leaving wine in a hot parked car during the trip.
  • Prioritise bottles that teach you something: an appellation, colour or grape that is genuinely hard to find in Australia.
  • Record the producer, cuvée and vintage so you can search the importer or a specialist retailer later.

Quick answers

What is the best base for a Provence wine trip?

Choose the base for the wine area, not the other way around. Aix suits Sainte-Victoire and Coteaux d'Aix; Marseille suits Cassis and western coastal visits; Hyères suits La Londe; and an inland Var base suits Coteaux Varois and central Côtes de Provence.

Do Provence wineries require appointments?

Some welcome walk-ins, while others require booking or use set tour times. Confirm opening hours, language, fees and reservations directly before travelling.

Can I visit without driving?

Yes, with a booked guide or driver, a carefully chosen accessible estate, or a planned public-transport and taxi combination. Do not assume every rural winery has a practical car-free connection.

How much wine can I bring back to Australia duty free?

When checked on 15 August 2026, the ABF allowance was 2.25 litres of alcoholic beverages for travellers aged 18 or over. Verify the current rule before departure and declare excess alcohol.
